# Project: PWHL overtime penalty-shot vs power-play decision model
Goal: CSAS 2027 student poster. Full plan in PROJECT_PLAN.md. Follow its steps in order.

## Conventions
- Python 3.12, pandas + pyarrow, PyMC + ArviZ for Bayesian models, matplotlib for figures.
- Raw data in data/raw is cached JSON and is never modified. Parsed data goes to data/interim, modeling tables to data/processed (parquet).
- All final logic lives in src/; notebooks are for exploration only.
- Every scraper must cache, resume and rate-limit (≤2 requests/second).
- Times are stored as seconds elapsed in the game; OT time remaining = 300 − seconds into OT.
- Any model reports posterior means with 90% credible intervals; check R-hat < 1.01 and zero divergences.
- Figures: colorblind-safe palette, large fonts (poster), saved as PNG at 300 dpi and SVG to outputs/figures/.

## Domain rules (PWHL)
- Regular-season OT: 5 minutes, 3-on-3, sudden death, then a shootout (5 rounds, then sudden death; shooters may repeat).
- OT minor penalty → 4-on-3 power play. Pilot rule: fouled team may instead take a penalty shot.
- No Escape rule (2024-25 onward): penalized team's skaters cannot change until after the next faceoff.
- Jailbreak: a shorthanded goal ends the penalty (in OT it ends the game anyway).
- Points: 3 for a regulation win, 2 for an OT/SO win, 1 for an OT/SO loss.

## Model decisions from exploration (Step 6, Nov 2026)
Evidence for each is in `notebooks/01_eda.ipynb`. Numbers below are from that notebook; recompute rather than copy them into results.
- **Penalty-shot model.** Fit the NHL first and use its posterior (α, σ_s, σ_g, widened) as the PWHL prior: the leagues convert shootouts at about the same rate. Expect small σ_s: shooter success is barely repeatable (split-half reliability near 0 in the PWHL, about 0.1 in the NHL), goalies more so; report that shooter differences are small. Keep δ (in-game penalty shot vs shootout) with a prior centered slightly negative, from the NHL. Round and home terms only as small NHL-informed adjustments, kept only if they help in the holdout test. **Outcome (Step 7):** the core model was kept; home/sudden-death and one pre-specified skill covariate (shrunk regulation shooting %) were tested once each and rejected by the rule. Do not try further covariates (forking paths). The usage pattern (regulars score more than the model expects) is a reported limitation, handled by the chosen-shooter sensitivity case in Step 10.
- **Power-play model.** PWHL OT power-play rate = league 5-on-4 rate × r, with r from the NHL 4-on-3/5-on-4 ratio (about 2.0, stable across seasons), then updated with PWHL OT power plays. League-level rate with a strongly shrunk team effect: PWHL teams show no detectable 5-on-4 differences. Scoring rate within a power play is two-piece, early (before the first stoppage) and late, with the early/late ratio's prior centered near the NHL value (about 1.15); the No Escape effect is modest, not a spike. Include a small shorthanded-goal rate, mostly from the NHL. **Outcome (Step 8):** base model kept; team effects and a No Escape change were tested once each and rejected (no further variants). The decision engine must add season-to-season variation in the league power-play rate as prediction uncertainty for a new season (2025-26 scored below prediction); never refit to the test season. The higher-than-predicted PWHL OT 4-on-3 scoring is handled by the r ±25% sensitivity case.
- **Overtime model.** The 3-on-3 scoring rate rises through overtime, so `W0(t)` uses a time-varying rate (linear in OT time or piecewise by minute), shape from the NHL and level from the PWHL, computed numerically on the 1-second grid. This replaces the constant-rate formula in PROJECT_PLAN.md §5.3. Home-ice term starts at no advantage and is only lightly updated by PWHL data. **Outcome (Step 8):** log-linear shape kept (per-minute rejected). The home term was made a candidate like every other term (it had been included by default) and was rejected in both leagues, so home and away score at the same 3-on-3 rate (50% home share). Team effects rejected. Shootout probability is computed exactly (not Monte Carlo).
- **Decision engine: penalty-shot δ cases (Step 9).** Under the pilot rule the team chooses its shooter, so historical in-game penalty shots (taken by whoever was fouled) are not a direct guide. Main case δ = 0 (chosen shooter, shootout-like); cautious case δ = NHL posterior (full historical gap); PWHL-updated δ (1 of 9 PWHL penalty shots) is reported as context only, never used for advice. Decided from the rule's mechanics before Step 10. All three are computed by `src/decision.py` (`delta=` argument).
- **Validation checks (not targets).** The model should reproduce NHL OT power-play conversion by time left (called with 2:00+ left vs under 2:00) from its structure, without being tuned toward it; if it doesn't, change the structure only for a stated reason and report it. The NHL backtest is leave-one-season-out so every prediction is out-of-sample. Matching Rachman (2026) confirms the data, not the model. Priors for NHL fits come from outside knowledge, never from our own exploration numbers. Full fitting and validation plan: PROJECT_PLAN.md §5.6.
- **Scope.** Preseason is excluded from the overtime models (most preseason OTs were not tied). NHL games with a missing goal are excluded from rates (it makes no difference, checked).
